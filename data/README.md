# Data provenance and calculation scope

The study uses the [OECD PISA 2022 database](https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/pisa-2022-database.html). Individual-level files are not distributed here. The included CSVs contain only country-level aggregates or coefficients transcribed from the thesis.

`derived/countries.csv` lists the 48 retained country IDs, the original country/region labels and record counts per imputation. Selection follows the retained thesis analysis sample, based on availability of the gaming and socio-emotional measures. Austria and Belgium lack observed emotional-control and trust scales. Country inclusion in this descriptive table does not certify inclusion in a fitted regression model.

In the surviving regression scripts, masks assign zero weight to Austria, Belgium and Costa Rica for all nine outcomes, leaving 350,422 positive-weight records in 45 countries per imputation. A positive-weight count is not a verified fitted-model sample: the original scripts do not explicitly filter these rows, and their exact execution remains unverified. The original R² calculations also omit these masks. The displayed historical coefficients consequently carry no claim of a newly confirmed sample size or model fit.

`derived/dpt_country_by_imputation.csv` contains 480 rows: one mean and record count for each country in each of ten preserved imputations. Its source is the thesis analysis file `R Syntaxen/Data/clean_data.csv`, SHA-256 `990d799e7d6d33eb542d6cd921d50205218fdad2619dab300dedd47fd1977805`. Means were calculated in a blockwise check of the full file. The original imputation has not been rerun, and values outside the coded playtime range have not been removed or clipped.

`results/tables/daily_playtime.csv` and Figure 1 are **portfolio recalculations**, giving the ten means within each country equal weight. The original recursive pairwise averaging assigned unequal weights to imputations; that step is corrected here. No confidence interval is inferred from the between-imputation spread. Country means are unweighted sample descriptions, not survey-weighted population estimates. Display labels retain PISA country/region terminology, including Ukrainian regions (18 of 27).

`results/tables/thesis_coefficients.csv` contains **historical thesis results**, not new estimates: the rounded standardised daily-playtime coefficients in Appendix A, Table 1 of Tony Willert's 2024 master's thesis, *The Relationship Between Digital Games and the Socio-Emotional Competencies of Adolescents: A Multilevel Analysis of the International PISA 2022 Data* (PDF pages 47–48). Comments in the surviving nine outcome scripts agree with those rounded values. The coefficients have not been independently refitted; no validated SE, p-values or confidence intervals are supplied.

The R measurement functions are adapted examples from the surviving preparation code, with input checks added. The R summary replaces the original aggregation step. The model file illustrates the research specification and performs no fitting. These are documented extracts and revisions, not recovered server production scripts.

The case study's argument also draws on Tony Willert's oral-defence presentation, *Disputation v.2*, including its speaker notes: slides 3–7 connect the time displacement rationale to the research question and methods; slides 11–17 develop interpretation, limitations and follow-up research. The presentation is used to condense the argument, not as an independent replication of the thesis results. Numerical findings remain sourced to the thesis and checked aggregate calculations described above. The original slides and private speaker notes are not redistributed here.

I describe the missing server production code and the retained materials in [my reproducibility statement](../README.md#reproducibility). See [methods](../docs/methods.md) for regeneration instructions and [limitations](../docs/limitations.md) for the scope of the surviving evidence.
